About

Ten years in the space between a CAD file and a finished alloy.

I'm a Research Associate in the Division of Engineering at New York University Abu Dhabi, where my work centers on architected metamaterials — mathematically defined Triply Periodic Minimal Surface (TPMS) lattices such as Gyroid, Schwarz Primitive, and Diamond topologies — built through additive manufacturing and pushed toward real structural and biomedical use.

A large part of that work lives inside Nitinol (NiTi) shape memory alloys: understanding how Laser Powder Bed Fusion parameters — laser power, scan speed, energy density — reshape the microstructure and phase transformation behavior of a lattice as it's printed, layer by layer. I hold a PhD in Materials Science and Technology from the Academy of Scientific and Innovative Research (AcSIR) at CSIR-AMPRI, Bhopal, and a BE in Mechanical Engineering from UIT-RGPV, also in Bhopal.

Before NYU Abu Dhabi, I spent four years as a Postdoctoral Fellow at Khalifa University, and earlier, as a Project Fellow at CSIR-AMPRI working on copper-based shape memory alloys via vacuum induction melting. Across that decade the throughline has stayed the same: computational design, additive manufacturing, and experimental characterization, increasingly tied together with Python and machine learning to predict how a material will actually behave before it's printed.

Research Focus

Three problems, one process-structure-property chain.

Everything below feeds the same question: how do design decisions and print parameters end up encoded in a material's final microstructure?

TPMS Metamaterials

Designing Gyroid, Schwarz Primitive, and Diamond lattices with tunable stiffness and energy absorption — architected structures whose mechanical response comes from geometry, not chemistry.

NiTi Shape Memory Alloys

Investigating how LPBF process parameters govern phase transformation behavior — the martensite-to-austenite switch that lets a printed lattice "remember" its shape — and where that transformation goes wrong.

Materials Informatics

Building Python and ML pipelines — SEM image segmentation, DSC transformation-temperature extraction, process-property correlation models — to turn characterization data into predictive tools.

Selected Projects

From lattice design to physical part.

Metamaterial Lattice Design

Computational generation of Gyroid, Schwarz-P, and Diamond TPMS unit cells with tunable relative density for structural and damping applications.

Design for Additive Manufacturing

Process-aware redesign of parts for metal LPBF — support strategy, orientation, and geometry adjustments to reduce residual stress and cracking.

Data-Driven Material Prediction

ML models trained on mechanical-test and DSC data to predict phase-transformation temperatures and structural performance ahead of physical testing.

Interpenetrating Phase Composites

3D-printed TPMS lattices impregnated with resin to create multiphase architected composites with enhanced damping and structural performance.

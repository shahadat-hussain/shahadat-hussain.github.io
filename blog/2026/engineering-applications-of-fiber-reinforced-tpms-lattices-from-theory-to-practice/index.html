The promise of TPMS (triply periodic minimal surface) lattices has always been their exceptional structural efficiency for lightweight energy absorption. But a critical question remained: How do chopped fiber reinforcement and thermal post-processing actually affect their mechanical response in real applications?

Our latest findings provide quantitative answers.

We systematically tested gyroid TPMS lattices across five polymeric materials—VeroWhite, Nylon 12, ABS, PLA-CF, and PPA-CF—at relative densities from 20-40%. The results are significant for design engineers:

Performance Metrics That Matter:

Annealed PPA-CF gyroids achieve specific energy absorption of 24-25 kJ/kg at densities of 200-430 kg/m³. To put this in context: this is comparable to metallic TPMS structures, but at substantially lower weight. For aerospace and automotive applications, that weight savings translates directly to efficiency gains.

The Processing-Performance Link:

Thermal annealing of chopped carbon fiber-reinforced lattices isn’t just a minor improvement—it’s transformative:

  • Peak stress increases by ~40%

  • Toughness improves by up to 60%

  • Post-yield deformation behavior fundamentally changes

But there’s a trade-off engineers must account for: our finite element analysis reveals elevated stress localization and increased plastic strains in fiber-reinforced lattices. This explains the experimentally observed post-elastic ductility trade-offs.

Application Prospects:

The figure above illustrates where these structures matter most:

  1. Aerospace: Structural damping and lightweight panels

  2. Automotive: Crash energy absorption systems and vibration isolation components

  3. Marine: Wave manipulation structures for drag reduction on ships and submarines

  4. Space: High energy absorption legs for soft landing of spacecrafts

  5. Civil/Mechanical: Vibration isolation and acoustic damping systems

The Design Takeaway:

Chopped fiber reinforcement combined with thermal post-processing doesn’t just incrementally improve TPMS lattice performance—it fundamentally modifies deformation behavior. This enables high-performance, lightweight architectures, but requires coordinated control of:

  • Relative density (we studied 20-40%)

  • Fiber reinforcement type and content

  • Thermal post-processing parameters

For engineers working on lightweight structural systems, the message is clear: fiber-reinforced, annealed TPMS lattices offer a viable path to metallic-level performance at polymer weight—but you must design for the altered stress distributions and post-yield behavior.

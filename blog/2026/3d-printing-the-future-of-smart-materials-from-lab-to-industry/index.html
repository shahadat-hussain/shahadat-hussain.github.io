The Challenge: Why Machining Nitinol is a Nightmare

Nickel-Titanium (NiTi) shape memory alloys are prized for their unique ability to “remember” shapes, high damping capacity, and biocompatibility. However, their high ductility and superior strength make them incredibly difficult to machine using traditional subtractive methods. Tooling wears out quickly, and complex geometries are often impossible to achieve—until now.

The Solution: LPBF & Architected Porosity

CAD models of architected lattices (a) Gyroid TPMS Lattice, (b) Primitive TPMS Lattice, (c) Gyroid Lattice Unit Cell, and (d) Primitive Lattice Unit Cell

Laser Powder Bed Fusion (LPBF) allows us to bypass traditional machining by building parts layer-by-layer. My recent research focused on a specific family of architected materials called Triply Periodic Minimal Surfaces (TPMS), including “Gyroid” and “Primitive” topologies. These structures aren’t just visually striking; they offer a massive surface-to-volume ratio and high strength-to-weight performance that is critical for industrial applications.

Key Industrial Takeaways

  • Sector Applications: These architected NiTi structures have transformative potential in Biomedical implants (matching bone stiffness to prevent stress shielding), Aerospace and Automotive (light-weighting and energy absorption), and Robotics (high-density actuators).

  • The “Energy Density” Threshold: For industry, process stability is everything. Our study found that surface quality is highly dependent on volumetric energy density. Specifically:

    • Primitive structures achieve even surfaces at a threshold of 48 J/mm³.

    • Gyroid structures require a ~12.5% higher threshold of 54 J/mm³ to prevent surface defects and pores.

  • Managing Material Loss: A critical industrial challenge identified was Nickel evaporation and the formation of oxide-rich phases. Since the Ni/Ti ratio dictates the material’s “memory” temperature, controlling laser power and scanning speed is vital to ensure the final part performs as intended.

  • Overcoming the Balling Phenomenon: A major hurdle in scaling LPBF is the “balling” issue—where the molten track breaks into spheres rather than forming a continuous weld. Our analysis shows that this occurs primarily at higher scanning speeds due to Plateau-Rayleigh instability. In industrial production, this creates high surface roughness and internal voids that can lead to premature structural failure. By fine-tuning the balance between laser power and speed, we can suppress balling to ensure the structural integrity of thin-walled TPMS lattices.

  • Design for Manufacturing (DfM): We observed that structural geometry directly influences the cooling rate. Gyroid lattices, with their higher surface areas, dissipate heat faster, which can lead to finer grain structures compared to primitive designs.

The Bottom Line for Industry

Additive manufacturing of TPMS lattices turns NiTi from a “hard-to-work” material into a programmable engineering tool. By optimizing laser parameters to the specific geometry of the part, we can now produce components with tailored porosity and functional performance that were previously unachievable.
